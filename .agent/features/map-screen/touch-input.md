# Map Screen Touch Input

> The DM panel works with a finger. Every drag, hit-test and context action in it — the Map Screen section, Exploration Mode, the fog and walls editor, and the Player Screen section's preview, layer rectangles and per-layer fog — is written once against Pointer Events, so a mouse, a finger and a pen all drive the same code; on top of that the stage takes a two-finger pinch to zoom and pan, a finger explores on the lift rather than the press so a pinch never toggles a door, a long press stands in for the right-click a touchscreen does not have, and hit targets grow to a finger's size while the drawn markers stay small. This is what lets a tablet or a phone run the session (`../remote-server/overview.md`), and it changes nothing for a DM on a mouse.

## Source files

- `src/views/pointerDrag.ts` — `trackPointerDrag` (one drag for mouse, touch and pen, listening on `document` and filtered by `pointerId`), `TAP_SLOP`, `GestureIntent`, `trackTapOrDrag` (a cursor acts on the press, a finger once the gesture is unambiguous), `LONG_PRESS_MS`, `onLongPress`, `blockWebkitGestures`
- `src/views/pinchZoom.ts` — `StageView`, `IDENTITY_VIEW`, `MIN_ZOOM`, `MAX_ZOOM`, `clampView`, `zoomAbout`, `panBy`, `trackPinch`, `trackWheelZoom`, and `attachStageZoom`, which wires the pair to a stage and its zoom button
- `src/views/MapExploreModal.ts` — the exploration stage's pinch/wheel zoom, the deferred exploration tap, long-press AoE removal, `blockWebkitGestures` on the modal
- `src/views/MapScreenPanel.ts` — `trackDrag(start: PointerEvent, onMove, onEnd?)`: the panel-side drags (pan preview, viewport rectangle, AoE and vision dots) over the same helper
- `src/views/MapFogModal.ts` — the fog and walls editor: the deferred press behind every walls tool (`natAt`, `placeWall`), the brush and marquee strokes, the long press that ends a wall chain, and the zoomable `dm-fog-inner` stage
- `src/views/DmControlPanel.ts` — the Player Screen section: the preview's look-around pan (middle button for a mouse, a plain drag for a finger), `makeDraggable` for the layer rectangles, `setupInlineFogDrawing` for the per-layer fog, and the long press that opens a layer row's context menu
- `styles.css` — the `Touch` block: `touch-action: none` on every drag surface, the `@media (pointer: coarse)` rules that grow the hit targets, and the `@container dm-panel` rules that lay a narrow panel out down its column
- `test/visual/harness/touch-entry.ts` — the harness page that runs the two gesture modules and the real stylesheet under a browser's own fingers

## Settings used

- none

## Requirements

1. Every gesture in the DM panel shall be driven by Pointer Events (`pointerdown`/`pointermove`/`pointerup`/`pointercancel`), so one implementation serves a mouse, a finger and a pen. Nothing in the Map Screen section, Exploration Mode, the fog and walls editor or the Player Screen section listens for `mousedown`, `mousemove`, `mouseup` or `mouseleave`. `contextmenu` stays where it is: a right-click is a mouse action, and the finger's equivalent is the long press of requirement 6.
2. `trackPointerDrag(start, { onMove, onEnd?, onRelease? })` shall attach its `pointermove`, `pointerup` and `pointercancel` listeners to `document` and ignore every event whose `pointerId` differs from the starting one, so a drag survives leaving the element and a second finger never steers it. It returns a function that detaches the listeners; `onRelease` runs whichever way the drag ends, including that teardown, and `onEnd` runs only on a real `pointerup`/`pointercancel`.
3. When the browser takes a gesture over and fires `pointercancel`, `trackPointerDrag` shall end the drag exactly as a `pointerup` does, so no drag or drag lock can outlive the finger.
4. Every element that starts a drag shall carry `touch-action: none` (`.dm-explore-overlay`, `.dm-explore-markers`, `.dm-explore-inner`, `.dm-map-preview-stage`, `.dm-fog-stage`, `.dm-layer-rect`, `.dm-fog-draw-canvas-inline`, the AoE/vision dots and handles, the viewport rectangles), or the browser would claim the gesture for a scroll and cancel the pointer stream mid-drag. The exploration stage itself shall not, so the AoE, Vision and Combat windows floating over it keep scrolling under a finger.
5. While the pointer is coarse (`@media (pointer: coarse)`), the marker hit areas shall grow to a finger's size through the `::before` inset (`-17px × var(--dm-map-zoom)`, against `-9px` on a fine pointer) without changing what is drawn, and the bar buttons (`.dm-explore-bar button`, `.dm-section-actions button`, `.dm-explore-open-btn`) shall be at least 44 px in both dimensions.
6. `onLongPress(el, run)` shall run `run` when a touch or pen pointer is held on `el` for `LONG_PRESS_MS` (500) without travelling more than 10 px, and shall not arm for a mouse, which has a button of its own. Lifting, cancelling or moving beyond the slop before the timer fires cancels it. It returns a disposer that clears the timer and detaches.
7. The Exploration Mode markers shall offer a long press as the touch equivalent of the right-click that removes an AoE (`fog-of-war.md` requirement 52), and the handlers shall be torn down with each `renderMarkers` and on close so a re-render leaves no duplicate.
7a. A marker on the map shall answer a press only while its own card is the open one in its list (`aoe-overlays.md` requirement 7a). A marker that is not the live one shall swallow the press and do nothing — neither move, nor be removed, nor let the press through to the map underneath — because a finger-sized hit area over a battlemap is hit by accident far more often than on purpose, and a map that rearranges itself while it is being read is worse than one that takes an extra tap to edit. The same reasoning places a rotation handle at least 48 px from its own anchor on screen, whatever the map's scale, and puts the live marker above it: at phone scale a small cone's tip falls inside the dot's touch target, and the finger could only ever rotate.
8. `blockWebkitGestures(el)` shall `preventDefault` WebKit's own `gesturestart`, `gesturechange` and `gestureend` on `el`, so a pinch inside the stage does not also zoom the whole Obsidian window on iOS and macOS Safari. Exploration Mode applies it to `modalEl` and drops it on close.

### The deferred press

8a. `trackTapOrDrag(start, { act, onMove?, onEnd?, intent?, slop? })` shall run `act` once: immediately for any pointer type other than touch and pen; for those, on the release when the pointer stayed within `slop` (`TAP_SLOP`, 12 px), or — with `intent: "stroke"` — as soon as it travels beyond it, passing the starting event so the stroke belongs to the point the finger landed on. `onMove` and `onEnd` shall fire only after `act` has run, so cancelling the returned disposer (what a pinch does) commits nothing.
8b. Every gesture that writes something on a press — the exploration door/room toggle, the fog brush and marquee, Wall, Door, Erase and Toggle door, and the per-layer fog strokes — shall go through it, so one rule covers the panel: a cursor acts on the press, and a finger acts once the gesture cannot still turn into a pinch or a pan.

### Pinch, wheel and the stage view

9. The exploration stage's view shall be a `StageView` of `{ zoom, offsetX, offsetY }` — the offset in pixels from the stage centre — applied to `.dm-explore-inner` as `translate(calc(-50% + offsetX px), calc(-50% + offsetY px)) scale(zoom) rotate(<map rotation>deg)`. Every client↔fog conversion reads the overlay's live bounding rect (`fog-of-war.md` requirement 45), so the zoom needs no maths of its own and hit-testing stays correct at any zoom.
10. `clampView` shall hold the zoom in `MIN_ZOOM`..`MAX_ZOOM` (1..6) and each offset within the overhang the zoom creates (`(zoom − 1) × size / 2`), so the map cannot be pushed off the stage and returns to centred at zoom 1.
11. `zoomAbout(view, nextZoom, anchorX, anchorY, width, height)` shall keep the content under the anchor in place — `offset' = anchor − (anchor − offset) × nextZoom / zoom` — so the map grows around the fingers or the cursor rather than the stage centre, and shall clamp the result.
12. When a second touch or pen pointer lands on the stage, `trackPinch` shall start a gesture from the current spread, midpoint and view, and call `onStart`. While two pointers are down it shall apply `zoomAbout` at the starting midpoint with the zoom scaled by `distance / startDistance`, then pan by the midpoint's travel, so a pinch zooms and two fingers moving together pan. It shall ignore a mouse and a single finger.
13. When a pointer is lifted or cancelled, `trackPinch` shall drop the baseline, so the next pair of fingers starts its own gesture instead of resuming from the old spread.
14. When a pinch starts, Exploration Mode shall abandon the exploration tap the first finger began (requirement 15), so no door is toggled and no room is revealed by a zoom.
15. When a touch or pen pointer presses the exploration overlay, the modal shall defer the door/room gesture (`fog-of-war.md` requirements 46–48) until the pointer lifts, and run it only if the finger travelled no more than 12 px and the gesture was not taken over by a pinch. A mouse or any other pointer type acts on the press as before.
16. `trackWheelZoom` shall zoom about the cursor on a `wheel` carrying `ctrlKey` or `metaKey` — which is how a trackpad pinch arrives — by `exp(−deltaY / 300)`, and `preventDefault` it. A plain wheel shall be left alone, because the windows floating over the stage scroll with it.
17. The Exploration Mode bar shall carry a zoom button (`dm-explore-zoom-btn`) before the Scale toggle, reading `Zoom: <n>%` and carrying `dm-fog-active` while zoomed in. Clicking it while zoomed in shall return the stage to fit (`IDENTITY_VIEW`); at fit it does nothing. Both stages get this button, and the trackers behind it, from `attachStageZoom`; each one passes only how its view becomes a transform, so the wording and the behaviour cannot drift between the two.
18. While the stage is zoomed, `.dm-explore-inner` shall carry `--dm-map-zoom` set to the zoom, so the marker dots counter-scale and their touch targets stay the same size on screen however far the map is zoomed in (requirement 5).
19. On close, the modal shall detach the pinch and wheel listeners and reset the stage view to `IDENTITY_VIEW`, so the next session opens at fit.

### The rest of the panel

20. The fog and walls editor's stage shall carry the same pinch, two-finger pan and ctrl/trackpad-wheel zoom as the exploration stage, through the same `attachStageZoom`, on a `dm-fog-inner` box the stage clips, with a `dm-fog-zoom-btn` in the tab row (`fog-of-war.md` requirement 19b). A pinch shall abandon the stroke in flight through `cleanupDrag` rather than commit it, and a long press on the overlay shall end a wall chain the way a right-click does.
21. The Player Screen section shall be usable with a finger: a layer rectangle moves under a touch drag, a long press on a layer row opens its context menu (`../image-layers/layer-controls.md` requirement 22), and the per-layer fog tools draw through the deferred press (`../fog-of-war/drawing-tools.md` requirement 11). The preview area itself shall keep scrolling the panel, so its look-around pan stays on the middle mouse button (`../dm-preview/pan-zoom.md` requirement 2a).
22. While the pointer is coarse, the fog editor's tab and toolbar buttons shall be at least 44 px in both dimensions and a range slider's thumb at least 28 px. The layer row's icon buttons shall grow in height only: they sit side by side in a narrow column, and a 44 px floor on both axes would push the row wider than the panel.
23. The Exploration Mode bar shall wrap (`flex-wrap`), because its seven controls at 44 px do not fit one row on a phone and would otherwise overflow the modal.
24. On a stage too narrow for the floating windows, the AoE, Vision and Combat panels shall become a tabbed sheet along the bottom of the stage (`fog-of-war.md` requirements 43c–43d), so the map keeps the whole screen until a panel is asked for. Its tab buttons are at least 44 px tall and the closed sheet takes no height.

### A panel narrow enough to be a phone

25. Nothing in the DM panel shall be reachable only by scrolling it sideways. A section header (`.dm-section-header`) wraps at any width, so its actions drop to their own row rather than being clipped — a panel can be narrower than its title plus three buttons at any window size.
26. While the panel's own width is at most 460 px (`@container dm-panel`, measured on the panel and not the window, because a side panel is narrow on a wide screen too), the section's actions shall take a full row under the title and share it, each button growing from its own label rather than from zero so that a row too narrow for three labels wraps instead of spilling them over one another, a join URL shall take its own row above its Copy button, and a section's primary action (`button.mod-cta`, the Choose Encounter button) shall be full width — the easiest target on a phone and the clearest hierarchy.
27. The existing `@container dm-panel (max-width: 380px)` rules continue to stack the combat tabs, drop the layer row's button column below its name, and tighten the D&D Beyond preview rows.
28. While the panel is narrow it shall carry a section jump bar (`.dm-section-nav`) as its first child, sticky to the top, painting no background of its own: it blurs what passes under it, because the panel sits on `--background-primary` in the main area and on `--background-secondary` in a sidebar, so any token painted there is the wrong shade half the time — a black band over a grey panel. Where `backdrop-filter` is unsupported it falls back to a surface and a rule. It holds one chip per `.dm-section` (`.dm-section-chip`, at least 44 px tall, sharing the width so none of them is reached sideways), carrying both a word from the section's key — Server, Player, Map, Combat — and that word's first letter, with the full title as its `aria-label`, because "Player Screen Server" and "Player Screen" read the same until the third word. A phone shows one section at a time, so the panel is a long column and jumping is the navigation it needs. The bar sits outside the session wrapper, so it keeps working while the session is read-only: it navigates, it does not edit.
29. Pressing a chip shall scroll that section to the top of the panel and unfold it if it was folded, because a folded section cannot be read. The chip of the section being read shall carry `dm-section-chip-active`: the first one when the panel opens, and on scroll the last section whose measured top has passed just under the bar — or, once the scroller has reached its end, the last section, because the sections at the bottom may never reach the top of a panel that runs out of scroll before them, and pressing Combat only to watch Server stay lit reads as a broken bar. The position is measured with `getBoundingClientRect`, and the scroll is caught on the document in the capture phase, because a scroll event does not bubble and on mobile neither the scrolling element nor the sections' offset parent is the panel — computing this from `scrollTop` and `offsetTop` left every chip unmarked. The listener is dropped on every re-render, so the bar never stacks up listeners, and it recomputes nothing while the bar is hidden.
28b. The blur shall belong to two scrim layers behind the chips (`.dm-section-nav::before`, `::after`) and not to the bar itself, because a mask on the bar would fade the chips with it. The near layer is a strong short blur and the far one a weaker blur reaching further down, each masked into its own ramp, so content scrolling under the bar goes gradually out of focus instead of crossing a line. The bar shall reach the panel's side edges (out through its padding), and the scrims shall reach up over the panel's top padding as well — sticky positioning cannot be pulled above the scrollport, so the bar itself stops below it — because a surface inset from the edges leaves a sharp strip of text down each side and another above, which reads as a box drawn around the chips.
28c. While the panel's own width is at most 300 px the chips shall show only their first letters — S, P, M, C — because four whole words need about that much room between them and a bar of clipped fragments navigates nothing. Both labels are in the DOM and the stylesheet picks one, and the `aria-label` is unchanged either way.
30. While the panel is narrow, each section shall be a card (background, radius, padding) rather than a hairline rule, the last section shall reserve `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` under it (asked of the section and not of the panel, because an element is never its own query container), and a press shall answer for itself (`:active` dims and shrinks the button slightly), since nothing hovers under a finger.

## Non-goals

- Touch in the Hydrus explorer, the calibration modal and the settings tab. Those are forms and lists a finger can already operate; nothing in them is a drag.
- Rotating the map with two fingers. Rotation stays the bar's 90° steps, because the map screen's rotation is a per-screen orientation rather than a free angle.
- A finger's look-around pan in the Player Screen preview. Claiming those drags would cost the panel its scrolling, and look-around only matters while the preview is zoomed out, where Reset View does the job.
- Zooming what the players see. The stage view is the DM's own magnifier; it broadcasts nothing, and the players' window is still moved by the viewport rectangle (`fog-of-war.md` requirement 51).
- Multi-touch on more than two pointers. A third finger invalidates the gesture rather than starting a new kind of one.
